RESEARCH PROGRAM 03

Marine Ecosystem & Biodiversity Intelligence

Study marine life, from coral reefs to microbes.

This program brings together ecological research by our colleagues in the Department of Ocean Science (OCES). Their projects study coral reefs, marine biodiversity and microbial communities in Hong Kong and the wider region, using field measurements, environmental DNA and molecular methods. Where it fits, the program connects this work with the marine AI and observation tools developed across CMI.

Our Ocean Science colleagues

  • Alex Wyatt ↗Coral ecosystems & marine biogeochemistry
  • Masayuki Ushio ↗Biodiversity, eDNA & ecosystem monitoring

SUB-DIRECTION 01

Coral Reef Ecosystems

Alex Wyatt’s group studies how ocean physics and biogeochemistry shape coral reef ecosystems. A central theme is internal waves, which can bring cooler, deeper water onto some reefs, and what this means for reefs as the ocean warms.

Research focus

  • Internal waves & reef refuges

    How internal waves affect temperature and conditions on reefs.

  • Reef biogeochemistry

    The nutrient and chemical conditions that reef communities experience.

  • Feeding & resource use

    What valuable marine species feed on and depend on.

Led by Alex Wyatt

Internal Waves & Coral Reef Climate Refuges

Internal waves can carry cooler, deeper water onto some coral reefs. This line of work studies their ecological impacts, the temperature and biogeochemical conditions they create, and whether such reefs can act as refuges from ocean warming.

  • Biogeochemical dynamics shaping internal-wave-driven coral reef climate refugesRGC General Research Fund · 2026–
  • Thermal variability and biogeochemical dynamics hidden in internal-wave refuges: validating a numerical ocean simulation at the global centre of marine biodiversityRGC General Research Fund · 2025–
  • Ecological impacts of internal waves on globally threatened coral reef ecosystemsRGC Early Career Scheme · 2020–2024

SUB-DIRECTION 02

eDNA & Biodiversity Monitoring

Organisms leave traces of their DNA in the water and sediment around them. Masayuki Ushio and Charmaine Yung develop and apply environmental DNA (eDNA) methods to monitor marine life in Hong Kong, from dolphins and reef fish to harmful algae.

Research focus

  • Water eDNA surveys

    Detecting which organisms are present from water samples.

  • Sedimentary eDNA

    Reconstructing past communities from DNA preserved in sediments.

  • DNA metabarcoding

    Identifying many species at once from a single sample.

Led by Masayuki Ushio

eDNA Monitoring of Cetaceans

Builds a framework for monitoring cetaceans, such as dolphins and porpoises, in Hong Kong waters using environmental DNA.

  • Establishment of an environmental DNA-based cetacean monitoring framework in Hong Kong watersRGC General Research Fund · 2024–2027

Past Coastal Communities from Sediment eDNA

Uses DNA preserved in seabed sediments to reconstruct how coastal ecological communities in Hong Kong have changed over time.

Harmful Algal Bloom Species

Uses DNA metabarcoding to study the diversity of harmful algal bloom species in Hong Kong coastal waters, and how they vary across space and time.

Coral Cover & Reef Fish Diversity from eDNA

Hong Kong’s turbid waters limit what diver surveys can record. This project uses eDNA from water and sediment to study coral and reef fish diversity in Hong Kong, and how the two are linked.

  • Linking coral cover and reef fish diversity in Hong Kong waters using eDNAHong Kong Offshore LNG Terminal · 2022–2024
Yung Lab research ↗

Charmaine Yung’s lab

eDNA Monitoring of Fish Restocking

For Hong Kong’s Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department, the lab collects water samples to follow juvenile fish of four species released at Yan Chau Tong Marine Park in September 2025, testing whether eDNA can show how the restocking is working.

Read the lab news ↗

A complementary record. Hong Kong’s waters are often turbid, which limits what divers and cameras can see. eDNA from water and sediment offers another record of which organisms are present. Comparing it with image-based observations is one possible area for future collaboration with Program 01.

SUB-DIRECTION 03

Marine Microbial Ecology

Much of the life in coastal seas is microscopic. Charmaine Yung and Stanley Lau study marine microbes, viruses and phytoplankton, how their communities change with environmental conditions, and how human activities affect them.

Research focus

  • Viruses & phytoplankton

    How viruses and grazers shape microbial and algal communities.

  • Coastal microbial communities

    How microbes change over time and across coastal environments.

  • Antibiotic resistance

    Resistance genes in marine fish and their surroundings.

Led by Charmaine Yung

Viruses, Grazers & Phytoplankton

Studies how viral infection and grazing by protists affect phytoplankton and microbial communities in subtropical coastal and estuarine waters, and how algae and their viruses have evolved together.

  • Exploring the temporal dynamics of protist grazing and viral lysis in regulating subtropical marine microbial communitiesRGC General Research Fund · 2025–
  • From genes to ecosystems: decoding the co-evolution of eukaryotic phytoplankton and nucleocytoplasmic large DNA virusesRGC General Research Fund · 2025–
  • The impact of viral lysis on coastal and estuarine eukaryotic phytoplankton under varying environmental conditionsRGC Early Career Scheme · 2021–2025
