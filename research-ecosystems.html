RESEARCH PROGRAM 03

Marine Ecosystem & Biodiversity Intelligence

Make marine life easier to see, count and compare.

This program connects the AI and observation tools developed across CMI with the ecological expertise of our colleagues in Ocean Science. AI researchers contribute methods for recognising organisms and organising large image and video records. Ecologists contribute the questions, field knowledge and molecular and environmental measurements needed to interpret them. Together, we aim to make biodiversity information easier to collect and compare, with ecological interpretation led by domain experts.

3 sub-directions

01From Imagery to Biodiversity RecordsTurning AI outputs into records ecologists can check and use. 02Ecological Knowledge & Molecular MonitoringThe ecological expertise our OCES colleagues bring. 03Linking Observations & Environmental RecordsReading images, water quality and samples side by side.

SUB-DIRECTION 01

From Imagery to Biodiversity Records

Our marine AI models can find, outline and describe organisms in underwater images. Here the aim is to turn those outputs into records that are useful for biodiversity work, such as which organisms appear, where and how often, with labels that specialists can review and correct.

What we study

  • Organism & habitat labelling

    Instance masks, species-level labels and coral growth forms from imagery.

  • Specialist review

    Interactive tools that let experts check and correct model outputs.

  • Comparable records

    Consistent labels so surveys from different sites and times can be compared.

Sai-Kit Yeung’s team

MarineInst: Understanding Individual Organisms

MarineInst recognises, segments and describes individual marine organisms in underwater images. It is built on MarineInst20M, a 20M-scale collection of instance masks with captions, and supports downstream tasks such as interactive labelling. Outputs like these can help ecologists find and count organisms across large image collections, although they still need expert checking.

SUB-DIRECTION 02

Ecological Knowledge & Molecular Monitoring

Images show only part of an ecosystem. Our colleagues in the Department of Ocean Science (OCES) bring expertise in coral ecosystems, microbial and plankton ecology, biogeochemistry and eDNA-based monitoring. This is the knowledge needed to ask meaningful ecological questions and to judge what observations can and cannot tell us.

Areas of expertise

  • Coral reef & microbial ecology

    Reef communities and the microbes that live in and around them.

  • eDNA & molecular monitoring

    Detecting organisms from DNA traces in water samples.

  • Marine biogeochemistry

    How carbon and nutrients move through marine systems.

  • Plankton ecology

    The small drifting organisms at the base of marine food webs.

Our ecologist colleagues

Who leads what. Ecological questions and interpretation are led by these colleagues. The AI and engineering side of CMI contributes tools that may make some kinds of data easier to collect, organise and analyse.

SUB-DIRECTION 03

Linking Observations & Environmental Records

Different kinds of evidence are easier to interpret when they can be read together. We are working towards linking image and video records with water quality, eDNA and other measurements collected at the same sites and times, so that domain experts can compare them more easily.

What we work on

  • Shared site records

    Visual, environmental and sample-based data organised by place and time.

  • Repeated observation

    Fixed cameras and repeated surveys that show change rather than snapshots.

  • Tools for domain experts

    Data views and AI-assisted summaries that ecologists can query and check.

Where this starts. The SeaSeeUST platform beneath the HKUST pier is being developed to collect underwater imagery and water quality records side by side. Its role in restoration research is described in Program 04.
